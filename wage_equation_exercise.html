Changing More Than One Independent Variable at the Same Time

Multiple regression, wage equation from WAGE1

1. The estimated equation

Using the WAGE1 dataset (n = 526), ordinary least squares (OLS) gives the following log-wage equation (Wooldridge, equation 3.19):

log(wage) = 0.284 + 0.092 educ + 0.0041 exper + 0.022 tenure
R2 = 0.316. Coefficients are semi-elasticities: a one-unit change in each x variable is associated with approximately (coefficient × 100) percent change in wage, holding the others fixed.

In section 3-2d, the question is: what happens when more than one variable moves at once? Because the model is linear in the parameters, you simply add up the contributions.

Δlog(wage) ≈ 0.092 · Δeduc + 0.0041 · Δexper + 0.022 · Δtenure

4. Practice problems

Compute the total effect on log wage for each scenario. Enter your answer as a decimal (for example, 0.0261, not 2.61%). Round to four decimal places. Then click Check.

Q1 A worker earns one more year of education while also gaining a year of general work experience but no additional tenure at the current firm. Compute the total change in log(wage).
Q2 A worker with 3 years of tenure at their current firm quits and starts fresh at a new firm. In the same year, they also gain one year of general experience. What is the total change in log(wage)?
Q3 A worker enrolls in an evening MBA (two years), keeps her job the entire time so exper and tenure both go up by 2, and educ goes up by 2. What is the total change in log(wage)?
Q4 Compare the two life paths from Q2 and Q3 above. Which produces a larger change in log(wage), and by how much? Enter the difference (Q3 answer minus Q2 answer), rounded to four decimals.
